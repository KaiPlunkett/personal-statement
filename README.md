# personal-statement

UCAS Personal Statements
------------------------
Statements are now three separate questions rather than one essay.
There is one 4,000-character budget (including spaces) across all three answers, with a 350-character minimum per answer.
The first question is "Why do you want to study this course?" The other two cover how your studies, and your experiences outside education, have prepared you.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1) Why do you want to study this course or subject?
----------------------------------------------------
Resident Evil helped inspire me to study Creative Media. Beyond the gameplay, I was drawn to how sound effects, unsettling music, silence and lighting combine to create tension and immerse the player. This sparked my curiosity about the creative and technical choices behind these experiences and motivated me to explore game development.
My passion for writing, performing and producing music deepened this interest. In a collaborative college game project, I created music, dialogue and sound effects, discovering how changes in tempo or instrumentation could transform a scene's atmosphere. Working with the art and narrative teams taught me the importance of making sound and visuals complement each other.
These experiences have reinforced my ambition to become an Audio-Visual Designer. I want to build my creative and technical skills, explore new production techniques and create immersive experiences that leave the same lasting impression on others that games like Resident Evil have left on me.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q2) How have your qualifications and studies helped you prepare for this course or subject?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Studying BTEC Level 3 Creative Media Practice (Game Design) at North Kent College has confirmed my enjoyment of both the creative and technical aspects of game development. Earning a Merit in my first year boosted my confidence, while our collaborative game project motivated me to challenge myself further.
I focused on sound and music, drawing on my longstanding interest in writing, performing and producing music. Using Logic Pro and Audacity, I composed original music, sourced suitable sound effects, and recorded and edited 112 lines of dialogue. Keeping dialogue clear and audio levels consistent proved challenging, but developing my mixing, mastering and normalisation skills helped me address these issues. Feedback from tutors and classmates guided further improvements.
Collaborating with the art, narrative and programming teams taught me that effective audio must support the game's visuals, characters and atmosphere, rather than simply reflect my musical preferences. I found it particularly rewarding to see how small sound adjustments could enhance the overall experience.
My studies have introduced me to Unity, Blender and Figma, broadening my understanding of digital media production. These experiences have reinforced my creative interests and my determination to develop my skills through higher education.
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Q3) What else have you done to prepare outside of education, and why are these experiences useful?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Outside college, I enjoy writing and producing music, from longer tracks to short stingers, and creating horror-inspired graphics to go with them. I want to work on horror games, so bringing these interests together helps me explore how sound and visuals can build atmosphere and tension. It also gives me a chance to think about how a piece of music's length and style can work with an image to create the same mood.
I like trying different art styles, including pixel art inspired by older Pokémon games and trading card designs. This encourages me to try new techniques instead of sticking to one style. Moving between these approaches also helps me think about how artwork communicates an idea and why one style might work better than another for a particular project.
Via self-paced learning, I teach myself programming languages and learn more about game development. Watching behind-the-scenes videos has helped me understand techniques such as 3D modelling and motion capture, and how they fit into making a game. Learning about these areas helps me see where audio fits into the process and which technical skills, I'd like to build on.
I'm currently putting together a portfolio of my music and artwork to show the skills I've developed so far. It brings my interests together and gives me a way to share work that reflects what I want to do. Working on these projects outside college keeps me motivated and has strengthened my ambition to pursue audio-visual design.
